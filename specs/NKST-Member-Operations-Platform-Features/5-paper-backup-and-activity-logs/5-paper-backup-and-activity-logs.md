## Overview

Paper Backup and Activity Logs keeps the physical ledger as evidence during the transition, marks records that also exist on paper, runs scheduled administrative backups, and stores every create, update, delete, and financial adjustment in an immutable audit trail. Authorized roles browse the trail with filters, and the historical hard cover note is digitized with the source of each entry tagged.

## Why It Exists

The PRD calls the hard cover note the sacred source of truth, so the transition cannot simply abandon it. This feature exists to keep the paper ledger as evidence, to prove the digital record can be recovered from backups, and to give the church an immunity to loss and dispute through an audit trail that no one can edit or remove.

## How It Works End to End

The paper linkage runs on the web portal. During the transition, the clerk keeps the physical ledger and marks the matching digital records as having a paper entry, so a record carries both the digital and the paper status. Administrative backups run on the agreed schedule and record their outcome, and the restore path is exercised so a backup can actually be recovered. Every create, update, delete, and financial adjustment anywhere in the platform writes an entry into the immutable activity trail, where no edit or removal path exists. Authorized roles browse the trail with filters for user, action, and date range. The historical hard cover note is digitized through migration screens where member related data is entered by the member or a clerk and church sensitive information is entered by the system admin, and each record carries a source tag for the entrant.

## Inputs It Consumes

The feature consumes the digital records and their paper linkage marks, the backup schedule and the outcome of each run, the events generated by every create, update, delete, and financial adjustment in the platform, and the historical ledgers entered during digitizing.

## Outputs It Produces

The feature produces the paper linkage marks on digital records, the backup outcome records, the immutable audit trail and the filtered review of it, and the digitized member records with source tags. The audit trail is consumed by every other feature as the proof of what happened, and the digitized records become first class members in the registry.

## User Journey Summary

Journey F in the PRD (`A finance admin reconciles the month`) touches the paper linkage in step, because the reconciliation view labels electronic and paper entries. Journey D touches audit logging, because every discipline step lands in the immutable audit trail. The digitizing path is new work that follows the PRD handover rule: member related data by the member or clerk, church sensitive information by the system admin, with the source tagged on each record.

## Feature-Level Acceptance Criteria

- A digital record that also exists in the physical ledger carries a paper entry mark during the transition.
- Administrative backups run on the agreed schedule and record their outcome.
- A create, update, delete, or financial adjustment anywhere in the platform produces an audit entry.
- An audit entry cannot be edited or removed by any role, including system admin.
- An authorized role browses the audit trail with filters for user, action, and date range.
- A digitized member record carries a source tag, with member related data by the member or clerk and church sensitive information by the system admin.

## Edge Cases and Risks

- A backup run fails. The outcome must be recorded as failed and visible, not silently missing.
- A restore is never tested. The PRD security rule requires the backup to be recoverable, so restore must be exercised.
- An actor tries to delete an audit entry. The system must have no delete path and must refuse the action.
- A digitized entry has no clear source. The migration screen must require the source tag before saving.
- The paper ledger retires later. Until the church retires it, the paper mark stays the default, per the PRD phase note.

## Dependencies

This feature depends on every feature that writes records, because the audit trail consumes events from `1-member-registration-and-identity`, `2-financial-contributions-and-giving`, `3-church-discipline-and-awards`, and `6-custom-modules-and-platform-features`. The paper linkage consumes the contribution and member records from the giving and identity features. Nothing else in the tree feeds on backup runs.

## Measurable Outcomes

The PRD ties the completeness outcome to this feature. From PRD `Section 5`: "Every record edit and every financial adjustment writes an immutable audit entry that cannot be edited or deleted." From PRD `Section 6`: "Full audit trail coverage on every record edit and every financial adjustment." No other metric is stated for this feature.
